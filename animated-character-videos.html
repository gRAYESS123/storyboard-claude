A cast that acts on cue

Characters are the fastest way to give an explainer a guide. In Easy Storyboard you place a character on a slide and give it a timeline of acts; it waves when the narrator says hello, looks at the chart being described, points at the number that matters and reacts when the punchline lands.

The built-in cast

  • Two people: person (modern flat) and cutout (construction paper). Choose skin (light, medium, tan, brown, deep), hair (short, long, curly, bun, buzz, cap, bald), hair colour and outfit colour.
  • Seven mascots: blob, orb, bot, cat, ghost, star and bean, in any colour.
  • Accessories: glasses, hat or bowtie.

Directing with a timeline

One attribute holds the whole performance. Times are seconds from the slide's start:

<div class="anim" data-anim="character" data-char="person"
     data-skin="tan" data-hair="curly" data-color="#EC4899"
     data-acts="0.5:wave; 2:look=#chart; 3:point=#chart;
                4.3:happy; 5.2:say=It doubled!"></div>
Act typeCommands
Gestureswave, nod, jump, bounce, shrug, spin
Moodsidle, happy, sad, surprised, think, wink
Gaze and pointinglook=#selector, point=#selector, rest
Speechsay=Text for a speech bubble (talks while it shows), talk, quiet

Characters also blink, breathe and sway on their own between acts, and lip-sync rides the voice-over when data-talk="1" is set. Because every pose is computed from the timestamp, renders are frame-perfect and you can scrub backwards and forwards freely.

Bring your own character

  • Visual rigger. Open rigger.html, paste your SVG, drag the eye and mouth handles into place, preview the moods and export the result.
  • JSON definitions. Register a character with Storyboard.defineCharacter(def) and check it with validate_character.py.
  • Tag your own art. Mark the parts of an existing SVG (eyes, mouth, arms) and the engine animates them.
